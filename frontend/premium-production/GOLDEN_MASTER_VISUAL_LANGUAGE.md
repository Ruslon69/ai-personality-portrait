# Golden Master Visual Language

Authority: approved `major-fool` candidate v1, `premium-tarot-style-v2`, artwork
checksum `8cccbb26fd91a70df31c3f2c0c5705d11a0ec4b8ca15a5383130864534e7aa9f`.
These rules describe the approved craftsmanship. They do not require other cards
to copy The Fool's sunlight, sky, palette, costume, camera, or spatial density.

## Production invariants

- Use premium hand-painted digital oil/gouache realism. Preserve deliberate brush
  variation at material transitions; avoid photographic smoothness and uniform
  AI airbrushing.
- Build subtle dimensional depth with perspective, overlap, occlusion, scale,
  value separation, edge control, and atmosphere. Never substitute background
  blur for spatial construction.
- Maintain natural anatomy, weighted posture, credible joints, and card-specific
  body types. Faces and hands must withstand 100% review without malformed,
  fused, waxen, mannequin-like, or generic beauty treatment.
- Motivate cinematic light from the depicted scene. Keep shadows readable,
  highlights protected, and bounce light material-specific. Antique-gold response
  is restrained and used only where the scene supports it.
- Control saturation. Establish one clear focal hierarchy and preserve the
  immediate legibility of every divinatory object, count, gesture, and
  relationship.
- Achieve collectible-card quality through craftsmanship, composition, tactile
  materials, painterly finish, and symbolic clarity. The application supplies
  the physical border, foil edge, bevel, and card shell.
- Reject any obvious generation artifact: repeated faces, malformed anatomy,
  accidental symmetry, fused objects, pseudo-text, incoherent materials,
  arbitrary glyphs, or detail that dissolves under inspection.

## Surface and material rules

### Painterly surface

Use fine-to-medium brush variation that follows form. Keep focal faces, hands,
and primary symbols precise; allow quieter edges and broader strokes in secondary
atmosphere. Do not apply a uniform texture overlay or sharpen every edge equally.

### Anatomy, faces, and hands

Construct the skeleton and weight-bearing pose before costume detail. Hands must
have correct digit count, joints, grip, foreshortening, and object contact. Faces
need card-appropriate age, asymmetry, bone structure, and subtle expression.
Across the deck, vary features, ages, skin tones, and body types intentionally;
never reuse one generic generated face.

### Fabric

Show weave, seam logic, thickness, gravity, compression, and construction-aware
folds. Silk, wool, linen, and ceremonial cloth must not share one glossy surface.
Costume color serves the card, not a deck-wide blue-and-gold uniform.

### Leather

Use worn grain, flex at joints, restrained edge polish, credible stitching, and
weight. Avoid plastic shine or identical brown material on every object.

### Wood

Show grain direction, joins, wear, porous end grain, and scene-appropriate finish.
Wands, tables, staffs, and structures must read as distinct built or natural wood.

### Stone

Use mineral variation, porous surfaces, tool marks, fractures, weathering, and
mass. Architecture must follow believable load, scale, and perspective.

### Metal

Separate aged gold, steel, silver, and bronze through value and reflection shape,
not saturation alone. Keep highlights controlled; Tarot tools must not look like
glowing RPG loot.

### Vegetation

Paint identifiable growth, stems, leaf structure, petal weight, and environmental
response. Vegetation supports symbolism and space; it is never decorative noise.

### Water

Show gravity, continuity, surface tension, refraction, reflection, wet contact,
and interaction with vessels or terrain. Luminous water remains physically
believable and never becomes glowing potion fluid.

### Sky and atmosphere

Choose weather and sky for the card's emotional logic. Use atmospheric
perspective to separate distance through value, hue, scale, and edge change.
Avoid galaxy wallpaper, generic purple fog, synthetic bloom, and repeated cloud
patterns across cards.

## Composition, light, and depth

- Establish the symbolic read at thumbnail scale, then reward close inspection
  with anatomy and material detail.
- Separate foreground, focal subject, midground, background, and atmosphere only
  where the scene needs them; iconic Minor cards may use a deliberately shallower
  structure.
- Keep canonical counts unmistakable. No atmospheric effect may hide a suit tool,
  vessel, sword, figure, star, gesture, or required horizon element.
- Use warm, cool, diffuse, storm, lunar, or ceremonial light according to the
  card. Coherence comes from restrained exposure and material response, not one
  repeated lighting setup.
- Reserve strongest contrast and sharpest edges for the primary symbolic action.
  Secondary detail must not compete with the card identity.

## Major and Minor Arcana

Major Arcana may use more monumental staging, broader environmental storytelling,
greater symbolic scale, and a slightly more cinematic camera. The environment
must still serve the canonical action rather than create unrelated spectacle.

Minor Arcana use faster symbolic readability and simpler composition when the
card calls for it. Simplicity is intentional, not lower effort: anatomy, material
rendering, light, painterly finish, and output quality remain identical to the
Majors.

## Permitted artistic freedom

Naturalize poses, perspective, spatial relationships, garments, architecture,
weather, and light when the canonical identity remains immediate. Adapt visual
density to the card. Vary camera height, environment scale, atmosphere, palette,
and material emphasis. Do not trace the public-domain scan or force every scene
into The Fool's high-altitude daylight.

## Forbidden style drift

- No anime, cartoon, childish fantasy, cyberpunk, hyper-neon, horror drift, or
  generic AI-fantasy spectacle.
- No repeated face template, wax skin, malformed anatomy, fused objects, extra
  limbs, pseudo-text, random occult symbols, or modern technology.
- No baked title, numeral, border, physical frame, watermark, logo, or UI.
- No indiscriminate particles, bloom, rarity effects, spell circles, or glowing
  object treatment.
- No deck-wide sameness in sky, blue/gold palette, sunlight, costume, camera,
  atmosphere, or composition density.

## Pilot coherence review

Review the seven pilots together. They must share craftsmanship, realism,
material specificity, anatomical quality, depth language, painterly finish, and
symbolic precision. They must not share one sky, light temperature, camera angle,
costume palette, atmosphere, or density. If coherence comes from copying The
Fool's scene rather than matching its execution quality, the pilot set fails.
